A CubeSat is a small-satellite standard developed at Cal Poly. CubeSats are measured in units (a "1U") of
10×10×10 cm, with a maximum mass of 2 kg per unit, and usually range from 1U to 12U. They launch as secondary payloads
or straight from the International Space Station, and are built mostly from commercial off-the-shelf parts. We use
grants and programs like NASA's CubeSat Launch Initiative to launch ours.

Originally developed in 1999 as an educational tool, CubeSats have grown into a platform used by universities,
startups, and space agencies to conduct science, test new technology, and enable commercial applications at a fraction
of the cost of traditional satellites. Because the bus is so standardized and can support many kinds of payloads,
they're far cheaper to build and launch. That opens up access to space for organizations that previously couldn't
afford it.
